How to use this index

Find the maker of your radio in the list. Open the maker's manual library link. Then search for your model on the maker's page.

Some popular manuals are linked right here. Tap the link to open the manual.

Every manual link carries a label that tells you what kind of file it is:

  • Text PDF — screen-reader-readable. This is a real text file. A screen reader can read it.
  • Scanned image PDF — not screen-reader-readable. This is a set of pictures of paper pages. A screen reader cannot read it. The words are pictures, not text.
  • Format not checked. We have not checked this one yet. It may be text or it may be scanned pictures.
  • Web page — lists manuals. This link opens a web page, not a manual. The manuals are listed on that page.

Some manuals are large files. A few are 30 megabytes or more. They open best on Wi-Fi.

Old radios live in the archive sections near the bottom of this page. The archives hold manuals for radios the makers no longer sell.

Icom

Icom keeps manuals for current and discontinued models in one searchable library. The library holds over 200 amateur radio documents.

                              mods.dk — the big manual archive

                              Most files here are scanned pictures, not text. Scanned image PDF — not screen-reader-readable. The manuals on mods.dk are mostly scans of old paper manuals. A screen reader cannot read them. The files are also mostly zipped. You download a zip file, then open it to find the PDF inside.

                              mods.dk holds over 13,000 manual files across about 153 brands. It is the first place to look for a radio the maker no longer sells.

                                mods.dk by brand

                                  RigPix

                                  Most files here are scanned pictures, not text. Scanned image PDF — not screen-reader-readable. RigPix holds user manuals and service manuals, mostly as scans of paper manuals. A screen reader cannot read them.

                                    BAMA — Boat Anchor Manual Archive

                                    Everything here is scanned pictures, not text. Scanned image PDF — not screen-reader-readable. BAMA is a library of scans of old paper manuals. A screen reader cannot read them. The BAMA site also asks for cookies to be turned on before it lets you download a file.

                                    BAMA holds manuals for hundreds of old brands, sorted into one folder per brand. The folders below are the ones our members ask about most.

                                      BAMA by brand

                                        DLARC — Internet Archive

                                        Most files here are scanned pictures, not text. Scanned image PDF — not screen-reader-readable. The DLARC collection is mostly scans of old books, magazines, and manuals. A screen reader cannot read most of them. A few items do have readable text. Each item page says what formats it offers.

                                        DLARC is the Digital Library of Amateur Radio and Communications. It is a project of the Internet Archive. It holds a very large collection of amateur radio books, magazines, and manuals, including Heathkit manuals.

                                          ManualsLib

                                          ManualsLib is a large general manual library. It covers far more than radio, but it holds many radio manuals too. Search it by brand and model.

                                            If a link is broken

                                            Links break. A maker may move a manual or take it down. If a link on this page stops working, try these steps.

                                            1. Try the Wayback Machine. It keeps old copies of web pages. Open the link below, paste the dead address into the box, and pick a saved date.
                                              • Try the archives. The same manual is often in more than one archive. Check mods.dk, BAMA, RigPix, and the DLARC collection.
                                              • Tell us. Post in the Visually Inconvenienced Hams group and say which link broke. The links on this page are checked every week, and a broken link gets fixed or replaced.

                                              About this collection

                                              This page links to manuals. It does not store them. The manuals belong to the companies that made them, or to the archives that scanned them. We do not copy or host the manuals here. Linking keeps you on the newest version the maker offers.

                                              We check every link on this page before it is added. We also check all the links once a week. If a maker takes a manual down, we point to an archive copy instead when one exists.

                                              Some makers keep old manuals online after they stop selling the radio. Others do not. That is why the archive sections matter. They hold the manuals the makers no longer offer.

                                              The page follows your device's light or dark setting, and the Accessibility button at the top opens the settings where you can pick either way. That choice is stored on your own device only and is never sent anywhere.
